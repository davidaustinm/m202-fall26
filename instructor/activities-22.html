    Worksheet Finance
    

    Remember that if you invest \(P\) dollars at an annual interest rate \(r\) compounded continuously, then after \(T\) years, you will have
    \begin{equation*} Pe^{rT}\hbox{ dollars.} \end{equation*}
    🔗

    1.

    Suppose that you invest $1000 at 7%. How much will you have after 10 years?
    🔗
    Solution.
    We’d have
    \begin{equation*} P = 1000e^{0.07(10)} = 2103\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    After you successfully finish Math 202, you get a job computing volumes all day, and you are able to save $10,000 a year (this is called an income stream).
    🔗
    Suppose that you will retire in 30 years, continually saving $10,000 a year and investing it in an account at an annual interest rate of 7%. We would like to determine how much money you will have at the end of 30 years.
    🔗
    Shown below is a representation of the 30-year interval during which you will be working. Time \(t\) represents the amount of time since you start working.
    🔗

    (a)

    How much money would you save (i.e. put into the account) during a 10-year interval? During a 2-year interval?
    🔗
    Solution.
    We save $10,000 per year so in 10 years, we would save $100,000. In 2 years, we would save $20,000.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    In the timeline above, a time interval of length \(dt\) is shown. How much money do you save during this time interval of length \(dt\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(dP=10000~dt\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    If you deposit this money \(t\) years after you start working, how long is it in the account before your retirement?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(30-t\) years.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    What is its value when you retire?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(dV=1000e^{0.07(30-t)}~dt\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    What is the total amount of money that you have when you retire? This is called the future value of the income stream.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} FV \amp = \int_0^{30}1000e^{0.07(30-t)}~dt\\ \amp = -\frac{10000}{0.07}e^{0.07(30-t)}\biggl|_0^{30}\\ \amp = 1,024,000 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (f)

    On the day you begin working, you learn that you’ve won the lottery. Your first thought is to quit work and invest the winnings at 7%. How much would you need to have won so that, after 30 years, you have the same amount you would have had if you worked and saved? This is called the present value of the income stream.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(Pe^{0.07\cdot30} = 1024000\) so \(P=125,363\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Let’s imagine a different scenario: the longer you work, the more money you make and hence the more money you save. For instance, suppose that you save at a rate of \(P(t) = 10,000+1000t\) dollars per year. How much will you have when you retire after 30 years?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} \int_0^{30}(10000+1000t)e^{0.07(30-t)}~dt = 2,058,000 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    If you would like to retire after 25 years with one million dollars, at what constant rate should you save money?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Call \(R\) the savings rate so that
    \begin{align*} \int_0^{25}Re^{0.07(25-t)}~dt \amp = 1000000\\ R \int_0^{25}e^{0.07(25-t)}~dt \amp = 1000000 \end{align*}
    so \(R=$14,772\) per year.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    Suppose that you save $5000 per year and you want to retire with one million dollars. How long do you need to work?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Let \(T\) be the amount of time that you work so that
    \begin{align*} \int_0^T5000e^{0.07(T-t)}~dt \amp = 1000000\\ -\frac{5000}{0.07}e^{0.07(T-t)}\biggl|_0^T \amp = 1000000\\ -\frac{5000}{0.07}(1-e^{0.07T}) \amp = 1000000\\ 1-e^{0.07T} \amp = -14 \\ T \amp = \frac{\ln(15)}{0.07} = 38.2 \end{align*}
    years.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6.

    Suppose that in your work for an oil company, you discover an oil field. Initially, it is easy to take oil from the field, but it becomes more difficult over time. Therefore, the rate at which you can extract oil is
    \begin{equation*} r(t) = 10 - 0.5t \end{equation*}
    millions of barrels per year.
    🔗

    (a)

    How long does it take to remove all the oil?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(r(t)=0=10-0.5t\) so \(t=20\) years.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    How much oil is there in the field?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} \int_0^{20}(10-0.5t)~dt = 100 \end{equation*}
    million barrels.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Assuming that the price of oil stays constant at $50 per barrel and an annual investment interest rate of 8%, what is the future value of the oil field?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} \int_0^{20}50(10-0.5t)e^{0.08(20-t)}~dt = 15.5 \end{equation*}
    billion dollars.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Suppose you want to sell the oil field. Why would you want to know the present value of the field? What is the present value?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(Pe^{0.08\cdot20} = 15.5\) so \(P=3.13\) billion.
    🔗
    🔗
