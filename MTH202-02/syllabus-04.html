    Section Section 04: Course outline

    Students sometimes come into a math class with expectations based on their previous experiences and general attitudes in our society. Here are some important facts that inform the way our class will be structured.
    • Math is social.
      Popular culture loves stories about a solitary genius who works for years in isolation. In reality, most professional mathematicians work closely with others and learn new ideas by talking with one another. This will be a feature of our class. One of your best resources will be your fellow classmates so I will help you get to know one another and learn to talk about mathematics. Not only will this help you learn math, but you’ll find it to be a lot more fun.
      🔗
      🔗
    • We learn by doing.
      I like watching professional basketball, but I still can’t make free throws. The best way to learn anything is to do it. This means that the majority of time in our class meetings will be devoted to students working through activities with their classmates (math is social, right?). This gives me a chance to get to know all of you as learners of math and to provide better feedback based on your understanding.
      🔗
      🔗
    • You belong in this class.
      Sometimes students can feel anxious in their math class. Have other students already had calculus? Does everyone remember everything from their high school math classes? Does everyone already know everyone else except me? These are pretty natural concerns but please know that I will do my best to get to know you and work with you with complete acceptance of your prior experiences.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Math makes sense.
      Math makes sense: Based on your previous math courses, you may think that the point of math is to get a "right" answer as fast as you can using some algorithm that no one really understands. Nope. Mathematics is a framework for making sense of our world, and our goal in this class is to develop your ability to think mathematically and to express your ideas clearly. A former student said, “Math is a lot easier when you think about what it means.”
      🔗
      🔗
    • I want you to succeed.
      The most satisfying part of my job is seeing my students grow as learners of mathematics and watching their confidence grow. I have high expectations of you in this course, but I will also provide you with the support that you need to reach those expectations. I am always interested in hearing about your experience, whether you are feeling frustrated or whether you want to share some recent success, and what kind of help you need from me.
      🔗
      🔗
    • What I expect of you.
      I’ve described some things above that I will provide you. Because learning is a partnership, I also expect some things from you because they will help you be successful.
      🔗
      Be open to new experiences and new people. Share freely with one another and accept the contributions of your classmates with gratitude.
      🔗
      Be willing to accept that you may not understand everything when you first see it but that I believe in your ability to work with me and your classmates to arrive at a deep understanding of mathematics.
      🔗
      Stay focused when working for this class. You may not pay monetarily to use social media, but you pay with your attention, which is one of your most valuable resources. Checking your phone regularly disrupts your concentration and significantly detracts from your learning. This is why the people in Silicon Valley who create this technology don’t let their kids use it. You may have a legitimate need to check your notifications during class, but you should otherwise be able to keep your phone in your pack for the 50 minutes that we meet.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    AI Policy.

    This is complicated, and we’ll talk about it in class, but please don’t use generative AI to complete any assignments or to check your work in this course. As full disclosure, I will tell you that I use AI a lot in a software project I’m involved in. This task is very different from actually learning mathematics, which is your job in this class. You may think:
    • “I will be using AI in my career.” With the current state of AI, you won’t be using it to learn new ways of thinking, like we’re trying to do in our course. AI will not help you develop an understanding of mathematics.
      🔗
      🔗
    • “I only use it to check my work when I don’t feel totally confident.” I am part of a math education research study that investigates how students learn math from textbooks. Interviews with students clearly show how using AI causes students to become less confident over time since they come to trust what comes from AI more than they trust their own work. Learning will be challenging and facing challenges requires you to have the confidence that you can do it. Please don’t surrender the trust that you have in your own abilities.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Here is one research study, among many others: Your Brain on ChatGPT. From the abstract:
    Over four months, LLM users consistently underperformed at neural, linguistic, and behavioral levels. These results raise concerns about the long-term educational implications of LLM reliance and underscore the need for deeper inquiry into AI’s role in learning.
    🔗
    🔗
    There’s also this from Nick Deanes, who graduated from GVSU with a math degree in 2016 and serves as Global Director of Talent Management at JR Automation, a company many of you would be thrilled to work for.
    🔗
    Majors and job titles change; the ability to think clearly, solve problems, communicate, and adapt doesn’t. Investing in these areas will always keep most opportunity doors open for you.

    Subsection About MTH 202

    MTH 202 is our second semester course in calculus, and you will start to see some more authentic, real-world uses of calculus. Some students have told me after this class, “Now I understand why people have told me I need to take math.”
    🔗

    Catalog Description​ of MTH 202, Calculus II.

    MTH 202 is a continuation of MTH 201 [Calculus I] using graphical, numerical, and analytic methods to study exponential, logarithmic, hyperbolic, and inverse trigonometric functions. Indeterminate forms, improper integrals, integration techniques, sequences and series, Taylor polynomials, and power series. Offered every semester.
    🔗
    🔗

    Prerequisites.

    ​ MTH 201 (Calculus I) or GV placement.
    🔗
    🔗

    Learning Objectives.

    After successful completion of this course, you will be able to:
    • Explain the First and Second Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, techniques and applications of integrals, series, particularly Taylor polynomials and series, and Differential Equations;
      🔗
      🔗
    • Interpret the interrelationships between information given in graphical, numerical, and symbolic forms;
      🔗
      🔗
    • Use communication skills, both verbal and written, in mathematics.;
      🔗
      🔗
    • Apply the concepts of integration to solve mathematical problems in geometry, physics and economics;
      🔗
      🔗
    • Apply quantitative and qualitative methods to differential equations to study applied problems such as population growth;
      🔗
      🔗
    • Determine an appropriate method to calculate a given integral;
      🔗
      🔗
    • Use an appropriate test to determine convergence of a given series.

    Subsection Assessment and how grades will be determined

    There will be several ways in which you will be assessed in this course and which will contribute to your final grade.
    Daily prep 10%
    🔗
    Before most class meetings, you will be asked to do some work to help prepare for our time together in class. This will often involve working a few short problems and providing me with feedback on your preparation. These will be graded most according to the effort you have put into them.
    🔗
    Homework on Runestone 15%
    🔗
    Our book is hosted on Runestone Academy and contains a free online homework system that you will use regularly to complete assignments. This will provide you with an opportunity for you to practice important skills required for success in this course and obtain immediate feedback on your work as well as the chance to attempt problems multiple times.
    🔗
    Learning targets 15%
    🔗
    We will have weekly proficiency quizzes addressing a set of Proficiency Learning Targets that measure your understanding of certain essential skills you need to build a deep understanding of calculus. Since this is an important part of the course, more details are provided later in this document.
    🔗
    Labs 10%
    🔗
    About every other week, you’ll start a lab or activity in class that you’ll complete and write up outside of class for a formal grade; normally these will be done in groups of two and assigned a single group grade.

    🔗
    There will be two in-class exams whose dates will be announced well in advance. Exams will be conducted without notes or computers.

    Subsection Learning targets

    This course has a set of learning targets that are intended to focus your attention on specific skills you should acquire to develop a deep understanding of calculus.
    🔗
    Here are the nuts and bolts of how it works.
    • Each week, we will have an in-class quiz that assesses some of these skills, which will be announced in advance.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Each skill will be graded on a four-point scale: “E’’ for “Exemplary’’ is four points, “S” for “Satisfactory is three points, and “NY’’ for “not yet’’ is zero points To earn an “E,’’ your solution needs to show all work and explanations, use correct notation, and arrive at a correct answer. In short, your work should be close to perfect.
      🔗
      🔗
    • If you did not successfully complete a learning target, you can try again next week. I won’t provide a lot of feedback because it’s very helpful for you to learn to assess your work independently of me. I am happy to talk with you about any of your work on the quizzes though.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Since you have multiple attempts on each target, there will be no makeup quizzes.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Each target will appear on two quizzes.
      🔗
      🔗
    • If you still haven’t successfully completed a target after your second attempt, you are welcome to talk with me about it during office hours (or another agreed upon time) within one week of the second attempt, and you can have another attempt.
      🔗
      🔗

      1. take responsibility for your own learning, and

        🔗
      2. seek help when you need support.

      Plagiarism.

      Any ideas or material taken from another source for either written or oral presentation must be fully acknowledged. Offering the work of someone else as one’s own is plagiarism. The language or ideas taken from another may range from isolated formulas, sentences, or paragraphs to entire articles copied from books, periodicals, speeches, or the writing of other students. The offering of materials assembled or collected by others in the form of projects or collections without acknowledgment also is considered plagiarism. Any student who fails to give credit in written or oral work for the ideas or materials that have been taken from another is guilty of plagiarism.
