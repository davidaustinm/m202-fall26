    Worksheet Normal distributions
    

    1.

    Let’s begin with the function
    \begin{equation*} p(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^2/2}, \end{equation*}
    which defines a ``normal distribution.’’ Plot this function and verify that it has the shape of a bell curve centered about the point \(x=0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    One inconvenience in dealing with this function is that it does not have an antiderivative in terms of functions that are familiar to us. Therefore, any definite integrals that we would like to evaluate involving this function require the use of a numerical method. That means that you’ll need to use Desmos to evaluate any definite integrals of this function.
    🔗

    (a)

    Since this is a probability density function, what should be the value of
    \begin{equation*} \int_{-\infty}^\infty p(x)~dx? \end{equation*}
    Verify that this is the case.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Using Desmos, we find that
    \begin{equation*} \int_{-\infty}^\infty p(x)~dx = 1 \end{equation*}
    as required for a probability density function.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    What is the probability that a data point will lie between \(0\) and \(1\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Using Desmos again,
    \begin{equation*} \int_0^1 \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^2/2}~dx \approx 0.341 \end{equation*}
    so about 34.1%.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Suppose that we measure the heights of men in the United States and find the normal distribution
    \begin{equation*} p(x)=\frac1{3\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-(x-70)^2/18} \end{equation*}
    where \(x\) is measured in inches.
    🔗

    (a)

    Plot this function (what is a reasonable range of \(x\) values?) and discuss the significance of the graph.
    🔗
    Solution.
    A reasonable range is roughly \(55 \leq x \leq 85\text{.}\) The graph is a bell curve centered at \(x=70\) inches, indicating that the average height is 70 inches, with a spread of a few inches on either side.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find the average height.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} \overline{x} = \int_{-\infty}^\infty x\cdot p(x)~dx = 70 \end{equation*}
    inches, the center of the distribution.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Then find the fraction of men whose height is between 70 and 72 inches.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} \int_{70}^{72} p(x)~dx \approx 0.248 \end{equation*}
    so about 24.8%.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    What fraction of men are taller than 74 inches?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} \int_{74}^\infty p(x)~dx \approx 0.091 \end{equation*}
    so about 9.1%.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    What is the median height?
    🔗
    Solution.
    The cumulative distribution function is
    \begin{equation*} C(s)=\int_{-\infty}^s p(x)~dx \end{equation*}
    and we want the median \(m\) that satisfies \(C(m)=1/2\) so \(m=70\) again. The median is the same as the average or mean.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (f)

    If \(\overline{x}\) is the mean of the density function, the standard deviation \(\sigma\text{,}\) which measures the spread in the data, is given by
    \begin{equation*} \sigma^2 = \int_{-\infty}^\infty (x-\overline{x})^2p(x)~dx. \end{equation*}
    Notice that this is defining \(\sigma^2\) so you’ll need to take the square root of the integral. Find the standard deviation of this distribution.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} \sigma^2 = \int_{-\infty}^\infty (x-70)^2 p(x)~dx = 9 \end{equation*}
    so \(\sigma = 3\) inches.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    The general form of the normal distribution is
    \begin{equation*} p(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{(x-\bar{x})^2}{2\sigma^2}}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Average annual snowfall in Grand Rapids is 72.4 inches with a standard deviation of 16 inches.
    🔗

    (a)

    A few years ago, we had a lot of snow with 109 inches recorded. What is the chance that there is more than 109 inches of snow?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} \int_{109}^\infty p(x)~dx \approx 0.011 \end{equation*}
    so about 1.1%.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    What is the chance that there is 40 or fewer inches of snow in a year?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} \int_{-\infty}^{40} p(x)~dx \approx 0.021 \end{equation*}
    so about 2.1%.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    What is the chance that the snowfall is more than the average?
    🔗
    Solution.
    By the symmetry of the normal distribution,
    \begin{equation*} \int_{72.4}^\infty p(x)~dx = 0.5 \end{equation*}
    so 50%.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    What is the chance that the snowfall is more than one standard deviation above the average?
    🔗
    Solution.
    One standard deviation above the mean is \(72.4 + 16 = 88.4\) inches.
    \begin{equation*} \int_{88.4}^\infty p(x)~dx \approx 0.159 \end{equation*}
    so about 15.9%.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
